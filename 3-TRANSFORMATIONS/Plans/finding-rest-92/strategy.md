# Finding Rest 92 — Strategy for a WeBuddhist Practice Plan

**A 92-day practice plan on chapters 1–8 of Longchenpa's *Finding Rest in the Nature of the Mind* — one day per khrid rkang (ཁྲིད་རྐང་, "guidance-step") of Longchenpa's own practice manual for the text — designed to turn a normal modern day into a mind-transformative experience using the WeBuddhist app's native features: notifications, timer, counter, shorts, and the feed.**

This document is the founding strategy for the plan. It defines what the plan is trying to change in the practitioner's mind at each stage, what the tradition says those changes are and how they are measured, and how each app feature is deployed — and constrained — in service of those changes. The binding session shape, schedule, and language streams will be distilled from this strategy into `About finding-rest-92.md` and the per-language contracts, following the vault's Plans convention.

---

## 1. The core idea

Longchenpa did not write *Finding Rest in the Nature of the Mind* as material for a weekly meditation session. The practice he prescribes is **all-day and all-night**: "So strive in this reflection day and night" (ch. 1); "Think night and day upon the utter certainty of death" (ch. 2); "Meditate by day on your root teacher up above your head / And meditate on him by night within your heart" (ch. 5); "Thrice by day and thrice by night, / Strive thus to cultivate the twofold bodhichitta" (ch. 8). Between formal sessions, perception itself is the practice: "Subsequently see all things as dream visions and illusions" (ch. 6).

Traditionally, the container for this total practice was the monastery, the retreat, or the village whose bells, liturgies, and calendar kept the reflections alive through the day. The modern practitioner has no such container — but they do carry a device that already structures their day, interrupts them dozens of times, fills their idle moments, and counts everything they do. **The strategy of this plan is to make the phone do for the modern practitioner what the monastery bell did for the traditional one.** Every feature the attention economy uses to fragment the mind — push notifications, timers, counters, short video, the infinite scroll — is retargeted to carry Longchenpa's day-and-night curriculum instead.

This is not a novelty move; it is classical Vajrayana logic. Dzongsar Jamyang Khyentse: "From the point of view of the Vajrayana, there is nothing that cannot be used as a method; and where there is wisdom, there is nothing that cannot be used as a path" (*Poison is Medicine*, ch. 9). The same book states the boundary: "the fundamental principles of Buddhadharma cannot and should not be adjusted in any way whatsoever" (ch. 5). The app is the cup; the dharma is the tea. We may design any cup we like. We may not change the tea.

## 2. Scope, audience, duration

**Scope.** Chapters 1–8 of the root text: the freedoms and advantages, impermanence, the sufferings of saṃsāra, karma, the spiritual master, refuge, the four unbounded attitudes, and bodhichitta. These are the chapters that require no empowerment and no formal entry into Vajrayana — the complete "reorientation of the mind" that the translators' introduction describes as the four mind-changes followed by the foundation of the Great Vehicle. Chapters 9–13 (generation and perfection stages, the view, meditation, and the result — khrid rkang 93–141 of the practice manual) are deliberately left for a successor plan for practitioners who complete this one and connect with a living teacher; the strategy in §8 explains how the plan points beyond itself without teaching what an app must not teach.

**Audience.** Modern seekers with no assumed Buddhist background — the app's "stressed professional," "spiritual seeker," and "curious student" personas. This choice creates the central design tension of the whole plan, because Dzongsar Khyentse is blunt about what such people usually want and why the dharma will not give it to them: "A closer look at why people engage in these practises reveals an aim that has little to do with liberation from delusion, and everything to do with their desperation to escape busy, unhappy lives" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 1). §4 defines how we resolve this tension honestly. A full `audience.md` per the vault template will be authored for the `en/` stream.

**Duration.** 92 days — and this number is not a design choice but the root text's own schedule. Longchenpa accompanied each volume of the Trilogy of Rest with a manual of practical instructions; for this text it is *The Excellent Path of Enlightenment* (*Byang chub lam bzang*, `1-SOURCES/Text/bo-don-khrid-byang-chub-lam-bzang.md`), which condenses the entire work into **141 numbered guidance-steps (khrid rkang)** — each a single, self-contained practice session, each closing with the formula "…this is the Nth guidance-step" (ཁྲིད་རྐང་…པའོ). The sūtra-vehicle section of the manual — Longchenpa's own distillation of chapters 1–8 — comprises **khrid rkang 1 through 92**; steps 93–141 cover Mantra and Dzogchen and belong to the successor plan. The plan therefore adopts the tradition's own pedagogical segmentation wholesale: **one day = one khrid rkang, and the day number *is* the khrid rkang number.** The practitioner who completes the plan has practiced, in order, every session Longchenpa prescribed for this stretch of the path — nothing added, nothing skipped.

**Name.** `finding-rest-92`.

## 3. What the text says the practice essentially is

Four structural facts about the root text drive the whole design.

**3.1 It is a progressive path where each stage produces the next.** Every chapter opens by referencing the attainment of the previous one — "So now you have your freedom, hard to find" (ch. 2); "Relying thus upon a spiritual master... Refuge is the sure foundation of all paths" (ch. 6); "When you are well practiced / In the four unbounded attitudes, / Meditate upon the twofold bodhichitta" (ch. 8). The stages are not a syllabus of topics but a causal chain of mind-states: joy-and-urgency makes death-awareness bearable; death-awareness makes the survey of saṃsāra honest rather than morbid; conviction in karma makes conduct meaningful; and so on. **Design consequence: the plan is strictly sequential. No skipping ahead, no à-la-carte browsing of later stages.** The feed and shorts serve only the practitioner's current stage.

**3.2 Each stage has an explicit, testable success measure.** Longchenpa states, in the text itself, what it feels like when a contemplation has worked: joy "like blind men who have chanced upon a treasure of great price" (ch. 1); "Rein in your projects for the future" (ch. 2); "If when it's explained you are not moved to sadness, / Your heart inert like iron or a piece of stone, / It's clear you have no mind at all!" (ch. 3); "The sign of your success will be that self and other, / Friend and foe are equalized" (ch. 7). These are the plan's real KPIs — inner, self-assessed, and traditional. The app's measurable metrics (streaks, counts, completions) are proxies and must never be presented as the goal (§4, principle 6).

**3.3 It prescribes a complete session architecture — and a per-day unit of practice.** The text repeatedly gives the same session skeleton: setting and posture ("In a clean and pleasant place... With crossed legs take your seat," ch. 8); the main visualization or contemplation; graded scaling ("first meditate upon a single being, / Then on two, then three... Then meditate on the entire universe," ch. 7); dissolution and rest ("At the end, dissolve the visualization step by step, / And relax just for a moment in the state of emptiness," ch. 5); dedication ("Then dedicate this virtue to enlightenment," ch. 5). The practice manual makes this architecture explicit *per unit*: khrid rkang after khrid rkang closes with the instruction to take up the contemplation "with the three parts complete — preparation, main part, and conclusion" (སྦྱོར་དངོས་རྗེས་གསུམ་ཚང་བས་ཉམས་སུ་བླང་ངོ་), and the very first khrid rkang already adds the between-session limb: dedicate afterward, "and in all conduct, apply the mind to this same contemplation" (སྤྱོད་ལམ་ཐམས་ཅད་དུ་ཡང་དེ་ལྟར་བསམ་པ་ལ་སྦྱར་རོ). Khrid rkang 10 names the post-session practice directly: the "yoga of conduct" (སྤྱོད་ལམ་གྱི་རྣལ་འབྱོར), done "again and again." Dzongsar Khyentse generalizes this as the three noble principles: arouse the motivation, do the practice with awareness, dedicate the merit — "If you can remember to apply them to all your daily activities, you will quickly become a great dharma practitioner" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 4). **Design consequence: this skeleton is hard-coded into the timer (§6.2) and echoed in miniature everywhere else, including the feed.**

**3.4 Its stated purpose is rest.** The prologue: "In the wilderness of karma and defilement / They stray in weariness. / Today I will bring rest to their exhausted minds." Every chapter's dedication returns to the refrain "May their minds today find rest," each time naming a specific exhaustion — weariness from defilements, from clinging to permanence, from "pursuing wrong, mistaken, and inferior paths." Longchenpa's own gloss on the title: the discursive mind's thoughts are "the travelers that, turning and turning for a long time in saṃsāra, wear themselves out," and must settle "in the state in which all concepts subside: the nature of the mind, their dwelling place of rest and ease." **Design consequence: the app's aesthetic and interaction language is rest, not stimulation. Every engagement loop in the plan terminates in stillness — the feed ends, the timer ends in silence, the day ends in dedication. This is the single clearest differentiator from every attention-economy product the user owns.**

## 4. The design conscience: seven binding principles from Dzongsar Khyentse

The two Khyentse books function as this plan's design review board. Every feature decision in §6 must pass these seven principles.

**Principle 1 — Not for happiness.** "It is such a mistake to assume that practicing dharma will help us calm down and lead an untroubled life... Dharma is not a therapy" (*Not for Happiness*, introduction). The plan never markets itself as stress relief, wellness, or self-care, and its copy never promises feeling better. "The aim of far too many teachings these days is to make people 'feel good'... if you are only concerned about feeling good, you are far better off having a full body massage."

**Principle 2 — The Coca-Cola strategy for meeting the audience.** We still welcome the stressed professional who arrives wanting calm. The method is Khyentse's own: help the town drink "one less bottle of Coke a day" — but "if you forget or fail to present the truth – that Coca Cola is full of sugar – you are no longer teaching Buddhism" (*Poison is Medicine*, ch. 5). Concretely: early-stage content may meet users in their stress, but the truth of the plan's actual aim — liberation, not relaxation — is stated plainly from day 1 and never softened. The onboarding says, in effect: this plan will not make your life more comfortable; it is designed to change what you want.

**Principle 3 — The cup is not the tea.** "In the same way that the cup and the tea are two separate entities, Tibetan culture, tradition and custom are completely different from the Dharma" (*Poison is Medicine*, ch. 4). App design, gamification mechanics, notification copy, visual style, language register — all cup, all freely adaptable, and we are consciously "creating a new culture." The meanings of the root text, the sequence of the path, the view — all tea, untouchable: "none of us can change one word of the Buddha's fundamental teachings and continue to call it Buddhism" (ch. 5). The vault's citation chain (§9) is the enforcement mechanism: every doctrinal statement in the plan traces through rails to the sources.

**Principle 4 — Fabricate until genuine.** "One of the first tasks for those new to the spiritual path is to revise your basic assumptions and fake it... Once any one of these emotions has been fabricated and contrived for long enough, it will automatically become genuine, which is why beginners must be content to base the majority of their practise on make-believe" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 1). This licenses the whole apparatus of prompted, scheduled, counted, artificially triggered reflection. A notification-induced thought of death is a contrived thought of death — and that is exactly what the tradition prescribes for beginners. The app should say this out loud; it disarms the objection that app-practice is "not authentic."

**Principle 5 — Consistency over quantity; the tortoise.** "The key is consistency... continue to follow your practise schedule precisely and consistently. This is how we can use our greatest enemy, habit, against itself" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 3). "Try to discipline yourself to practise every day instead of just once in a while; or practise for half an hour every day, instead of five minutes one day and an hour the next" (ch. 4). And on commitments: "never to pledge too much at the beginning... Far better to make more flexible promises that you then keep" (ch. 14). Design consequence: small default commitments, escalating by consent; streak mechanics reward regularity, not volume.

**Principle 6 — The sign of no-sign.** "We will have no experiences, no special dreams, no pure visions. The 'king of all signs'... is when renunciation mind, sadness and devotion blaze in your mind" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 14). And: "the only true indicator of your spiritual progress is whether or not your devotion for your guru and your compassion for sentient beings increases" (ch. 6). Design consequence: progress screens foreground the stage measures of §3.2 via journal self-assessment, and explicitly relativize the countable metrics. The counter's purpose "is not merely the accumulation of numbers, but to penetrate our minds, ruffle the feathers of our pride and make a satisfying dent in our egos" (preface).

**Principle 7 — Methods may be thrilling; the view seals them.** "No matter which method you choose to practise... it must go against, oppose and counter duality" (*Poison is Medicine*, ch. 8). "Mindfulness practice without the view is just another of samsara's boring games" (ch. 8). Every practice unit in the plan — session, scroll, count — carries at least a seed of the view appropriate to its stage (illusoriness, dream-likeness, nonreferential rest), so that the plan never degenerates into technique. This is also why every contemplation in ch. 7 ends nonreferentially: "Then with a mind completely free of concepts, / Rest within the ultimate condition, empty and unborn."

## 5. The stage map: eight transformations in 92 days

The stage boundaries and day counts below are not editorial. They are the manual's own topic divisions (དོན་, *don*): Longchenpa opens each topic with its enumeration — impermanence, for instance, is announced as twelve contemplations (བསམ་པ་ལ་བཅུ་གཉིས་) and occupies exactly khrid rkang 11–22. Days map one-to-one onto khrid rkang.

| Stage | Days (= khrid rkang) | Chapter / manual topic | Transformation goal | Traditional measure of success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The Treasure | 1–10 | 1. Freedoms and advantages | Felt astonishment at one's opportunity; urgency joined with joy | "Like a beggar who has chanced upon a treasure of great price, / Reflect with joy upon your freedoms and advantages" — joy plus striving "day and night" |
| 2. The Hourglass | 11–22 | 2. Impermanence | Certainty of death that loosens the grip of future-planning | "Rein in your projects for the future— / Better far to strive in Dharma from this very instant!" |
| 3. The Waterwheel | 23–38 | 3. Sufferings of saṃsāra | Honest, felt disillusionment with every samsaric strategy, high or low | "If when it's explained you are not moved to sadness... It's clear you have no mind at all!" |
| 4. The Shadow | 39–46 | 4. Karma | Conviction in infallible cause and effect, expressed in conduct | Treating nonvirtues "as the enemies they are"; adopting and rejecting without sliding into nihilism |
| 5. The Guide | 47–55 | 5. The spiritual master | Trust in guidance; reorientation from consuming content to following a path | "All existence / Will arise as your own teacher... You will live from day to day, quite free from cares" |
| 6. The Ground | 56–58 | 6. Refuge | Relocation of basic trust from failing refuges to the Three Jewels | "Even in their dreams, they see the objects of their refuge / And never part from them" |
| 7. The Four Doors | 59–64 | 7. Four unbounded attitudes | Systematic dismantling of the friend/enemy/stranger partition | "Self and other, / Friend and foe are equalized"; per-attitude signs (love like a mother's, unbearability of others' suffering, envy-free joy) |
| 8. The Vow | 65–92 | 8. Bodhichitta | Identity reconstituted around the awakened intention | "'From this day forward, I will be / The ground of sustenance for every being. / I assume the name of bodhisattva'" |

The weighting is Longchenpa's own, and it corrects intuitions a modern course designer would get wrong. The four mind-changes (stages 1–4, days 1–46) occupy exactly half the plan — everything after them is only as real as they are; a person who has not felt stages 2 and 3 will practice stage 8 as sentimental decoration. But the single longest stage is bodhichitta (28 days, nearly a third of the plan), because in the manual it carries not just the vow but the whole training of the six perfections, including concentration and wisdom. And refuge — which a modern designer would stretch into a fortnight of content — is three days of contemplation whose *practice* then runs as a daily thread to the end (§6.3): the manual marks it "the ground of all dharmas, of great importance — apply yourself to it earnestly" (ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་གཞི་མ, khrid rkang 58).

Stage transitions are **gates, not page-turns**: on the last day of each stage the practitioner meets the stage's traditional measure as a journal self-assessment (e.g., after stage 2: "This week, did you actually drop or shrink any project because you remembered you will die?"). The gate can be crossed regardless of the answer — honesty, not performance, is the criterion, per the sign of no-sign — but the question is always asked, and the answer is kept for the practitioner's own review at day 92.

### Per-stage character notes

**Stage 1 — The Treasure (days 1–10).** Ten contemplations, from the freedoms themselves through the pit of the lower realms, the similes of rarity, the tragedy of waste, and — the closing khrid rkang — supreme joy. The plan's tone is set here: not "welcome to your mindfulness journey" but "you have found something almost impossibly rare, and you are almost certainly wasting it." Longchenpa's three grades of human life give the mirror: the "superior" human, "utterly distracted by their busy occupations," is the app's own persona described seven centuries early. Contemplations work the treasure images (the blind man, the beggar, the boat: "If, now that you have found a precious boat, / You fail to cross saṃsāra's shoreless sea..."). Feature emphasis: notifications establish the day-and-night rhythm; the timer introduces the 10-minute session; the feed teaches its own inverted grammar (§6.5). The counter is not yet introduced — accumulation before motivation would be "spiritual tax" (Khyentse's phrase for parrot-fashion practice).

**Stage 2 — The Hourglass (days 11–22).** The manual's own "twelve contemplations of impermanence" — the aggregates, the lords of beings, the outer world, the holy beings, the uncertainty of the time of death, the nature of compounded things, the causes of death, going alone, the people of the past, and so on — one per day. Death-recollection, made personal and constant. This is where notification design carries the most weight, because Longchenpa's instruction — "Think night and day upon the utter certainty of death" — is precisely an interruption practice: death-awareness must arrive *inside* ordinary activities, not in a session about death. Modern hooks from the text: the tended body as "the future food / Of jackal, fox, and graveyard bird"; "Tomorrow death awaits them, but they think they'll live forever"; the century that removes "most of those who now are on this earth." The gate question is behavioral (see above), because the text's measure is behavioral: reduced projects.

**Stage 3 — The Waterwheel (days 23–38).** Sixteen days surveying saṃsāra — realms, mechanisms, and the three sufferings — pointed constantly at the practitioner's actual strategies: career, wealth, relationships, pleasure. The text's own sensory-capture verse is the stage's centerpiece and the app's most self-referential moment: "like moths caught in candlelight... By sound, by scent, by taste, by touch are beings caught" — displayed, with full intent, inside the feed that catches by sight and sound (§6.5). Khyentse's "heart of sadness" frames the stage's affect: sadness as "one of the most invaluable kinds of noble wealth," not depression — the plan must keep this distinction explicit, and stage copy includes his caution against indulgence: "If you feel the need to indulge your sufferings, do it alone." The dream-teaching gives the stage its seed of the view: sufferings "appear and yet are nonexistent," like dreams — and no one else can wake you.

**Stage 4 — The Shadow (days 39–46).** Karma as the shadow that "cannot be transferred to others," in the manual's eight steps: the nonvirtues of body, speech, and mind with their results, then the virtues mirrored back, then the karma tending toward liberation. The stage turns contemplation into conduct: daily adopt-and-reject micro-commitments drawn from the ten virtues/nonvirtues, checked in the evening review. The counter debuts here in its simplest form — counting kept commitments — before it becomes a mantra/accumulation counter in stages 6–8. The stage must hold the text's balance: causality is infallible *and* dreamlike ("Actions, when examined, are without intrinsic being. / Yet they make, as in a dream, all kinds of joy and sorrow"), against both nihilism and the modern bypass Longchenpa already skewers: the yogi who calls karma "provisional teaching fit for children."

**Stage 5 — The Guide (days 47–55).** The hinge stage, handled with restraint (§8). The manual's nine steps run from the reasons for reliance through the qualities of authentic and inauthentic teachers to the yoga of the teacher — including a continual day-and-night yoga, the activity practices, and, as the topic's final khrid rkang, the practice for the time of sickness and death. Its transformation goal in an app context: the shift from *consuming spiritual content* to *following guidance* — trust, discernment of authentic teachers (the text's criteria, including the warning about charlatans "Even if they have great followings" — follower-count as false credential is a teaching made for the social-media age), and the aspiration to find a living teacher. The app explicitly names what it is not: "Guru yoga is the only way of building a bridge between ourselves and the nature of our mind" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 6) — and an app is not a guru. Practice: the four pure attitudes (self as sick, dharma as medicine, teacher as doctor, practice as therapy), gratitude toward the lineage that produced the text, and Longchenpa's day/night presence practice adapted as presence-of-the-teaching.

**Stage 6 — The Ground (days 56–58).** Refuge as the relocation of trust — three days of contemplation in the manual (the divisions of refuge, its benefits, and the way of going for refuge), followed by a *practice thread that never stops*: from day 58 to day 92, the refuge formula runs as a daily accumulation alongside whatever the current stage teaches, honoring the manual's note that this is "the ground of all dharmas." The contemplation days inventory the practitioner's actual refuges — savings, status, relationships, health regimes, the phone itself — against "All other refuges deceive," and Longchenpa's dedication for those "Exhausted through relying / Upon evil objects, vile and false" (burnout, named in the 14th century). Formal practice: the refuge visualization and formula, "Repeat this many times and from the bottom of your heart" — the counter's first accumulation practice, with the Khyentse/Jigme Lingpa horizon kept visible: refuge is complete when "the refugee and object of refuge become inseparable." Post-session integration: "Subsequently see all things as dream visions and illusions. / Then dedicate your merit."

**Stage 7 — The Four Doors (days 59–64).** Six days, one khrid rkang each: the benefits of the four attitudes, then impartiality, love, compassion, joy, and finally the training in alternation that rotates them. The order is the text's own — beginning, as it insists, with impartiality: the friend/enemy/stranger partition is the stage's target, and the modern material is endless (polarization, in-group feeds, envy as the engine of social comparison). The text supplies a complete progressive method made for graded app sessions: "first meditate upon a single being, / Then on two, then three. / Beginning with the beings of one region..." — a literal expanding-circles UI. Each attitude has its own sign (the stage's daily self-checks), each corrects the excess of the previous (a rotation the plan schedules), and each session ends nonreferentially: "Rest within the ultimate condition, empty and unborn." Sympathetic joy gets special weight as the direct antidote to the feed-native emotion of envy: "What joy! These beings have no need of me / To bring them to the state of happiness!"

**Stage 8 — The Vow (days 65–92).** Generation of bodhichitta in intention and action; the plan's culmination and the practitioner's change of identity: "I assume the name of bodhisattva." Khyentse supplies the stakes: "without a comprehensive appreciation of bodhichitta, all Buddhist practise degenerates into spiritual materialism" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 8) — this is the stage that retroactively protects the other seven. The manual's 28 steps give the arc ready-made: the preliminaries and the seven branches one by one (days 65–72), the generation of bodhichitta itself around day 73 as a marked event, prepared like Longchenpa's ritual ("In a clean and pleasant place adorned with offerings"); the precepts of aspiration and action; exchange of self and other and giving-and-taking (tonglen) "turn by turn"; then the six perfections in the manual's own sequence — giving, discipline, patience, diligence, concentration (with its contemplations of solitude and the faults of busyness), and finally wisdom, so that the plan's last days (90–92) are trainings in emptiness that end, on day 92 itself, with resting in the Middle Way free of extremes and the post-session dedication "in the state of dream and illusion" — the practitioner left exactly at the threshold where chapters 9–13 begin. The six-session rhythm, "Thrice by day and thrice by night," is the stage's notification architecture; the three noble principles wrap every activity. The plan ends not with a completion certificate but with dedication of the whole 92 days and the pointer forward (§8).

## 6. Feature strategy: five modern tools, retargeted

The general pattern for every feature: **find the traditional practice it accidentally imitates, then make the imitation deliberate.** Each feature below gets its traditional basis, its per-stage deployment, its degeneration risk, and its safeguard. All content served by every feature is stage-locked (§3.1) and rails-cited (§9).

### 6.1 Notifications — the monastery bell

**Traditional basis.** The day-and-night reflection instructions of every chapter (§3), plus Khyentse's explicit modern rendering: "You can discipline yourself to think of bodhichitta every time you walk through a door, or hear a phone ring" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 7). The notification *is* the phone ringing — the trigger practice with the trigger built in.

**Deployment.** Three registers, tuned per stage:
- **Anchor notifications** (fixed, 2–3/day): morning motivation (stage-specific verse fragment + intention), midday interruption (the stage's contemplation compressed to one line), evening dedication call. In stage 8 this expands toward the six-session rhythm.
- **Ambush notifications** (randomized timing, the stage's genius move): especially stage 2, where an unpredictable "You will die. When is unknown. What matters right now?" at a genuinely arbitrary moment enacts "Uncertain is its when and where and how" in a way no scheduled session can. Randomness here is not an engagement trick; it is doctrinally load-bearing.
- **Contextual notifications** (opt-in, tied to user-declared routines: commute, lunch, bedtime): e.g., stage 6 bedtime refuge ("Calling them to mind by day and night"), stage 5/8 sleep-practice prompts adapted from Khyentse's sleep yoga ("transform the mundane activity of sleeping into something worthwhile").

**Degeneration risk.** Khyentse names it precisely: "whatever you choose to use as a reminder will quickly become old and degenerate into another meaningless ritual. So keep your practise fresh by constantly changing your reminder" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 7). **Safeguard:** a large rotating copy pool per stage (the day-files' notification blocks feed it), no notification text repeated within a stage, and periodic form-breaking (a notification that is only a bell sound; one that is blank; one that asks the user to put the phone down).

### 6.2 Timer — the session container

**Traditional basis.** The session skeleton of §3.3, plus Khenpo-approved brevity: "even if you only practise for ten minutes a day, by devoting two or three to cutting the chain of thoughts, your mind will be completely transformed" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 4).

**Deployment.** The timer is not a blank stopwatch; it is a **structured session player** — the delivery vehicle for the day's khrid rkang, whose three-part formula (preparation, main part, conclusion — སྦྱོར་དངོས་རྗེས་གསུམ) the manual repeats at the close of almost every guidance-step. Its phases mirror Longchenpa's architecture: (1) settling and motivation (the first noble principle; ~1 min); (2) the stage's main contemplation, guided or silent, with graded scaling where the text prescribes it (stage 7's expanding circles); (3) dissolution — a bell, then explicit release of the contemplation, "relax just for a moment in the state of emptiness"; (4) dedication (~30 sec). Default 10 minutes, adjustable; the dissolution-and-rest phase is the one phase that can never be disabled, because it is the plan's homeopathic dose of the view (Principle 7) and the enactment of the title: the session literally ends in rest. Silence, not music. In stage 8, the timer offers the six-session micro-mode: 2-minute bodhichitta sessions, "Thrice by day and thrice by night."

**Degeneration risk.** Sessions as self-improvement units — "recharging batteries" mindfulness, which "without the view... is just another of samsara's boring games." **Safeguard:** the motivation and dedication phases frame every sit inside the path; session copy never references stress, focus, or performance benefits.

### 6.3 Counter — the mālā

**Traditional basis.** Accumulation and repetition are native to the path — "Repeat this many times and from the bottom of your heart" (ch. 6) — and the counter is a digital mālā (108 to a "round"). Khyentse's frame for what counting is *for*: "not merely the accumulation of numbers, but to penetrate our minds, ruffle the feathers of our pride and make a satisfying dent in our egos" (preface); and his flexibility: a teacher may prescribe accumulation "by time — four months, or one hundred hours" rather than by number (ch. 14).

**Deployment.** Introduced late and by stages (nothing to count until there is motivation to count with): stage 4, counting kept adopt-and-reject commitments (conduct, not recitation); stage 6, refuge formula accumulation in rounds of 108; stage 7, per-attitude session rounds; stage 8, bodhichitta verse and tonglen rounds. Counting UI mimics the physical gesture (a swipe per repetition — the thumb on the bead), supports time-based mode as a first-class alternative, and displays counts *within* the stage only — no lifetime vanity totals on the profile.

**Degeneration risk.** The counter is the feature most prone to Khyentse's "spiritual tax... rattling off your prayers parrot-fashion," and to pride in numbers. **Safeguard:** periodic quality interrupts ("Stop. Was that from the bottom of your heart? Count only those."), no leaderboards ever, no social sharing of counts, and the sign-of-no-sign framing on every accumulation summary.

### 6.4 Shorts — reverse conditioning

**Traditional basis.** Khyentse's startling observation about small screens is the design brief verbatim: "Our habits are so entrenched that having programmed ourselves to respond to a specific kind of image, it will consistently have the power to turn us on or make us angry, sad or even depressed, even when we see it on a tiny 14 x 8 cm YouTube screen. To a certain extent, this is how visualisation works" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 5). Short-form video conditions the mind through repeated charged imagery. The tradition's name for deliberate conditioning through imagery is visualization: "The imagination is therefore one of our most powerful tools" (ch. 5). Shorts are visualization practice for people who don't know they're doing it.

**Deployment.** Per-stage shorts of 30–90 seconds in a small set of repeatable formats: a single verse fragment, voiced, over one strong image (the blind turtle, the bubble, the moth and the candle, the waterwheel); micro-teachings extracted from the commentarial layer once its rails are laid; contemplation prompts that end on a held silent frame; stage-6+ guided micro-visualizations (the refuge field as a *seen* image, built cumulatively across days — pure perception in training). Repetition is a feature, not a bug: the same core images recur across a stage because conditioning requires repetition — this is also Khyentse's boredom advice ("when you find your mind wandering off in search of something new, just switch your focus") applied as rotation within a fixed stage set, not novelty.

**Degeneration risk.** Dharmatainment — shorts optimized for shareability drift toward "feel good" content (Principle 1) and toward the teacher-as-performer dynamic Khyentse skewers. **Safeguard:** shorts carry no like counts and no external share loop during the plan; the format budget above is closed (no reaction content, no personality-driven formats); every short traces to rails.

### 6.5 The feed — doom scrolling, inverted

**Traditional basis.** The text's diagnosis of capture: "like moths caught in candlelight... Like deer, like bees, like fish, like elephants, / By sound, by scent, by taste, by touch are beings caught" (ch. 3) — the five senses as capture devices is a literal description of the feed. And the text's diagnosis of the underlying problem: "What is it that prevents us from being free? Distraction" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 4). The feed is where the plan fights on the enemy's terrain: the practitioner *will* scroll something at 11pm; the strategic question is only what the scroll does to their mind.

**Deployment.** The feed is the plan's **post-meditation integration surface** — the "between sessions" limb of §3.3 — and it obeys four structural rules that invert the doom-scroll:
1. **Stage-locked:** it serves only current-stage content (shorts, verse cards, journal prompts, review cards of earlier days in the stage). No algorithmic surprise. The practitioner's feed *is* their curriculum.
2. **Finite:** it ends. After a bounded number of items (a "round," ~10–15), the feed terminates in a rest card — a plain screen with a dissolution instruction ("Let it all go. Rest for three breaths.") and no further content below it. Scrolling past the rest card closes the feed. This is §3.4 as interaction design: every loop terminates in stillness. An infinite dharma feed would be a betrayal of the text it teaches; "finding rest" must be literally what the feed does.
3. **Self-aware:** especially in stages 2–3, the feed periodically shows the moth-and-candle verse, or "Tomorrow death awaits them... They are borne away by busyness and pastimes," *as feed content* — the mirror inside the mechanism. This self-reference is the plan's honest answer to the paradox of building a scroll to end scrolling.
4. **Dream-tagged:** from stage 6, occasional interstitials carry the post-meditation instruction itself: "See all things as dream visions and illusions" — training the practitioner to hold *all* appearances, including the feed, as dreamlike.

**Degeneration risk.** The obvious one: that "dharma scrolling" becomes a pious skin on the same compulsion, recharging the doom-scroll habit with self-congratulation. **Safeguards:** the finiteness rule is absolute (no "load more"); session-length analytics are watched for compulsive patterns and answered in-product (a user scrolling many rounds a day gets stage-appropriate confrontation, not more content); and the feed is closed during timer sessions and for a configurable window after the evening dedication.

### 6.6 Streaks, journal, progress (supporting mechanics)

The **streak** rewards the tortoise (Principle 5): it counts days on which *any* plan practice occurred, never volume, and it bends rather than breaks — Khyentse's "flexible promises that you then keep" translates to a repair mechanic (a missed day can be acknowledged with a short confession-and-restart practice, modeled on the confession branch of the seven branches, rather than nuking the count; the honest acknowledgment is itself practice). The **journal** is private, prompted by the day-file's reflection question, and is where the stage gates (§5) live; day-92 review plays the practitioner's own gate answers back to them — the plan's real progress report. The **profile** shows the stage map, not charts; its language is the language of the eight exhaustions and rests, not achievement.

## 7. The transformed day

What the plan feels like from inside, at cruising altitude (a stage-3 day — day 31, whose khrid rkang is the contemplation of the four rivers of birth, aging, sickness, and death — for a working practitioner):

**07:10 — Anchor notification.** A verse fragment: "Pleasure to find and pain to flee is all that they desire, / Yet sorrows do they chase in cause and fruit." One line of intention-setting. Ten seconds.

**07:25 — Timer session (10 min).** Settling and motivation; today's khrid rkang — the four rivers of birth, aging, sickness, and death, worked through the practitioner's own body and family; bell, dissolution, a moment of rest; dedication.

**08:40 — Commute (contextual, opt-in).** Today's short: the waterwheel image, 45 seconds, voiced verse over animation, ending on a silent frame.

**13:05 — Ambush notification.** "There is no certainty in kinship, / No count of friends that into enemies have turned." Nothing to tap. It is only there to be met.

**Idle moments.** The feed, when opened, serves the stage: a verse card, yesterday's contemplation in one line, a short, a prompt ("What did you reach for today when discomfort came?") — then the rest card. The feed ends. The phone, for once, stops first.

**21:30 — Evening anchor.** Review prompt in the journal (two minutes), then the dedication verse of chapter 3 — "For beings wandering through these six domains... Exhausted by so many sorrows, may their minds today find rest" — and the feed closes for the night.

Total formal practice: ~15 minutes. Total practice: the day. That ratio — the day itself as the practice surface, with formal sessions as anchor points — is the strategy in §1 delivered, and it is Khyentse's definition of the real thing: "The heart of dharma practise does not lie in the quantity of mantras we chant or how long we spend in meditation, but in simple, everyday activities."

## 8. What the plan must not do: the boundary of the medium

Three restraints define the plan's integrity as strongly as anything it does:

**No Vajrayana content.** Chapters 9–13 presuppose empowerment, samaya, and a living teacher. The plan teaches *about* their existence (stage 5, and the day-92 pointer) but never their content. "Keep it secret, keep it safe" is not nostalgia; it is method protection.

**No simulated guru.** The app delivers teachings; it must never occupy the teacher's seat — no parasocial teacher-personality, no AI guru, no language implying the app knows the practitioner. Stage 5 teaches the qualities of authentic teachers precisely so the practitioner can find one *off* the app. The plan's honest self-description: a container and a courier. "Aspiring dharma practitioners today are very fortunate. They have at their fingertips a huge amount of information about the dharma... But for those who are serious about following a spiritual path, information is just a small part of the story" (*Not for Happiness*, ch. 6).

**No wellness drift, ever, including in growth copy.** Marketing, App Store text, and re-engagement campaigns are part of the plan's speech and bound by Principle 1. A re-engagement notification that says "Your calm is waiting" would undo the curriculum it interrupts. The correct register for winning back a lapsed practitioner is the text's own imagery — closer to "the precious boat is still here" (ch. 1) than to any promise of calm.

## 9. Production pipeline in this vault

The strategy above is generated from direct study of the sources; the plan's day-by-day content must instead flow through the vault's citation chain (`1-SOURCES/ → 2-RAILS/ → 3-TRANSFORMATIONS/`). Day files may not cite sources directly. The dependency order:

1. **Sources are in place** — the Padmakara translation, the Tibetan root text and auto-commentary, the practice manual (`1-SOURCES/Text/bo-don-khrid-byang-chub-lam-bzang.md`), *Not for Happiness*, *Poison is Medicine*, and the ngöndro commentaries are already in `1-SOURCES/` (some still to be formatted per the `format-root-text` / `format-commentary` skills, with block IDs). The manual needs khrid rkang–level block IDs; the working segmentation in `0-INBOX/raw-data/` is nearly there, but two units (khrid rkang 45 and 67) sit merged inside neighboring lines and must be re-split against the manual's own closing ordinals before the file is finalized.
2. **Lay rails for chapters 1–8**: the khrid rkang units are the schedule's backbone, so each of the 92 units needs a context package linking it to the root verses it condenses; plus section summaries (`section-summary-raw`, `section-summary-combined`), verse-context packages (`verse-context`) for the verses the schedule selects, local-wiki articles for the plan's key terms, and the bo-en glossary via the glossary skills. Only `status: complete` rails feed generation.
3. **Translate the manual**: the don khrid has no English translation in the vault, and the en day files' session instructions derive from it — a Translation track for khrid rkang 1–92 (rails-based, per the Translations convention) is therefore on the critical path before day-file generation.
4. **Author the plan contracts**: `About finding-rest-92.md` (binding session shape distilled from §§5–7), then `en/requirements.md`, `en/audience.md` (from the template, per §2), `en/termbase.md` (via `glossary-select`), `en/schedule.md` (92 rows: day = khrid rkang number, stage, manual unit, root verse refs).
5. **Generate day files** (`days/day-1.md` … `day-92.md`), each carrying its khrid rkang content and its notifications block (anchor + ambush + contextual copy for the rotation pools of §6.1) — the Plans convention already anticipates exactly this.
6. **App integration**: day files and notification pools export to the Plan Studio CMS; the feed's stage-locking and rest-card behavior, the structured timer phases, and the counter modes are product requirements to hand to the WeBuddhist team alongside the content.

A Tibetan (`bo/`) stream and other languages follow the same contracts independently, per the Plans convention.

## 10. Success criteria

In order of authority:

1. **The traditional measures** (§3.2), self-assessed at the eight gates and reviewed at day 92. The plan succeeds if practitioners report, in their own journals, reined-in projects, the heart of sadness, softened enmities, and the wish to find a teacher and go on.
2. **Path continuation**: practitioners who finish and seek a living teacher, a sangha, or the successor plan — the plan's real conversion metric.
3. **Consistency metrics** (completion rate, streak stability) — read as proxies for Principle 5, never surfaced to users as goals.
4. **Engagement metrics** — explicitly subordinated: a *decrease* in total time-in-app across the 92 days, with stable daily touch-points, is a success pattern, not a failure. An app teaching this text should expect to be needed less as it works. "Do not constantly aim to finish the practise. Instead, try to accept that your spiritual journey will never end."
