# Finding Rest 92 — Curriculum

The day-by-day curriculum of the plan defined in `strategy.md`. One day = one khrid rkang (ཁྲིད་རྐང་, "guidance-step") of Longchenpa's practice manual *The Excellent Path of Enlightenment* (*Byang chub lam bzang*), in the manual's own order: khrid rkang 1–92, the complete sūtra-vehicle section corresponding to chapters 1–8 of *Finding Rest in the Nature of the Mind*. The day number is the khrid rkang number.

**Provenance and status.** The Tibetan topic phrases below are verbatim from the manual. The English titles and day-work summaries are *provisional working renderings* prepared directly from the Tibetan for curriculum planning — they are not the translation. All user-facing day-file content must be generated through the vault's citation chain (khrid rkang rails + the en Translation track) before publication. Two segmentation anomalies in the source file are flagged in §Production notes at the end.

**The anatomy of every day.** The manual prescribes the same three-part session for nearly every khrid rkang — "practice with preparation, main part, and conclusion complete" (སྦྱོར་དངོས་རྗེས་གསུམ་ཚང་བས་ཉམས་སུ་བླང་ངོ་): preparation = refuge and bodhichitta; main part = the day's contemplation; conclusion = dedication, often with a moment of rest. Many units add the between-session instruction: apply the same reflection "in all conduct" (the yoga of conduct), "again and again." The app renders this as: the timer session delivers the three-part session; notifications and the feed carry the day's contemplation into the rest of the day; the evening anchor closes with dedication and journal. Feature mechanics are specified in `strategy.md` §6 and are not repeated per day here.

## Stage 1 — The Treasure · days 1–10
**Chapter 1: The freedoms and advantages · manual topic 1 (dal 'byor)**

**Transformation goal:** felt astonishment at one's own opportunity — urgency joined with joy. **Measure:** "Like a beggar who has chanced upon a treasure of great price, / Reflect with joy upon your freedoms and advantages." **Gate question (day 10):** "This week, did you catch yourself treating an ordinary day as something rare? What did you do with it?"

| Day | Session | The day's work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The eight states without freedom**<br>དང་པོ་ནི། མི་ཁོམ་པའི་གནས་བརྒྱད་བསམ་པ་སྟེ། | Contemplate the eight unfree states one by one; rejoice that you are in none of them, and resolve to use the freedom you have. |
| 2 | **The ten advantages**<br>དེ་ནས་ཁྱད་པར་འབྱོར་པ་བསམ་པ་ནི། | Check off the five personal and five circumstantial advantages in your own life — all ten are presently complete — and resolve to make them meaningful. |
| 3 | **The abyss below**<br>དེ་ནས་ངན་འགྲོའི་གཡང་ས་བསམ་པ་ནི། | Contemplate what follows if this support is wasted: states where no teaching is heard and nothing can be told apart; feel the drop beneath the present moment. |
| 4 | **The blind turtle and the yoke**<br>དེ་ནས་ཐོབ་དཀའི་དཔེ་ལ་བསམ་པ་ནི། | Work the similes of rarity: the blind turtle surfacing once a century into a drifting yoke; the handful of peas thrown at a wall, of which scarcely one sticks. |
| 5 | **The arithmetic of rarity**<br>དེ་རིམ་པའི་རྣམ་དབྱེ་བསལ་བ་ནི། | Contemplate the graded scarcity: among beings, humans; among humans, those who meet the Dharma; among those, the few who practice. Locate yourself in the count. |
| 6 | **Empty-handed from the isle of jewels**<br>དེ་ནས་དོན་མེད་ཆུད་ཟོས་བསམ་པ་ནི། | You have already landed on the jewel island. Contemplate the tragedy of returning empty-handed — a life of distraction with the treasure in reach. |
| 7 | **The rare conjunction of conditions**<br>དེ་ནས་རྒྱུ་རྐྱེན་རྟེན་འབྲེལ་བསམ་པ་ནི། | Dharma depends on mind, mind on this body, this body on conditions now assembled — health, independence, freedom from crushing suffering. The assembly is temporary; act now. |
| 8 | **The wheel of birth and death**<br>དེ་ནས་སྐྱེ་ཤིའི་འཁོར་ལོ་བསམ་པ་ནི། | Contemplate how many times this fortune has been gained and squandered before; resolve: "this time I will make it meaningful." |
| 9 | **What this body can attain**<br>དེ་ནས་དལ་འབྱོར་གྱི་བསྔགས་པ་བསམ་པ་ནི། | Every awakening — śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha, buddha — is attained on this support and no other. Praise of the free human body; seize the start of the path. |
| 10 | **Supreme joy**<br>དེ་ནས་སྤྲོ་དགའ་མཆོག་ཏུ་བསམ་པ་ནི། | The stage's culmination: look at your own life like a pauper who has found a jewel — "is this a dream?" — generate great delight, and resolve to practice. Gate day. |

**Working the stage:** notifications establish the day-and-night rhythm with rotating verse fragments on rarity and waste; the feed teaches its finite, rest-terminated grammar from day 1; the counter is not yet introduced. Day 10's joy contemplation is deliberately the first gate — the stage fails if it produces guilt instead of delight.

## Stage 2 — The Hourglass · days 11–22
**Chapter 2: Impermanence · manual topic 2 — Longchenpa's "twelve contemplations of impermanence" (བསམ་པ་ལ་བཅུ་གཉིས་)**

**Transformation goal:** certainty of death that loosens the grip of future-planning. **Measure:** "Rein in your projects for the future— / Better far to strive in Dharma from this very instant!" **Gate question (day 22):** "Did you actually drop, shrink, or start any project this fortnight because you remembered you will die?"

| Day | Session | The day's work |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | **This body will be scattered**<br>དོན་གཉིས་པ་ཚེ་མི་རྟག་པ་བསམ་པ་ལ་བཅུ་གཉིས་ཏེ། | The body now fed, clothed, and adorned will lie naked in an empty place, carried off piece by piece. Practice this very moment. |
| 12 | **Even lords of beings die**<br>དེ་ནས་སྐྱེ་དགུའི་བདག་པོ་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | Indra, Śiva, the great sages — beautiful, aeon-lived, miraculous — all die. "How then shall I not die?" |
| 13 | **Worlds end**<br>དེ་ནས་སྣོད་བཅུད་ཆགས་འཇིག་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | The physical universe itself is formed and destroyed — seven fires, one flood. Nothing built lasts; nothing housed in it lasts. |
| 14 | **Even the holy have passed**<br>དེ་ནས་དམ་པའི་སྐྱེ་བོ་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི་ | The seven Buddhas of the past came, taught, and departed; even their teachings wax and wane. How could we and our circle be permanent? |
| 15 | **The time is unknown**<br>དེ་ནས་ནམ་འཆི་ངེས་མེད་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | Life is consumed moment by moment like a rope gnawed by two mice; the place and time of death are unknowable. "When will I die?" |
| 16 | **The nature of compounded things**<br>དེ་ནས་འདུས་བྱས་ཀྱི་རང་བཞིན་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | Once-flourishing cities stand ruined and empty; the body is a town, the mind a mist passing through it. When does it empty? |
| 17 | **A lamp in the wind**<br>དེ་ནས་འཆི་རྐྱེན་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | The burning lamp is quickly consumed — and a single gust can end it now. Illness, accident, harm: none of it schedules an appointment. |
| 18 | **Going alone**<br>དེ་ནས་གཅིག་པུ་འགྲོ་བ་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | The last bed, the last meal, the last words — then leaving everyone and everything, utterly alone. "When will this come to me?" |
| 19 | **Generations like harvests**<br>དེ་ནས་དུས་ཀྱི་སྐྱེ་བོ་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | Of everyone alive a century ago, none remain; of everyone alive now, scarcely one will remain in a century. Recall the vanished people of your own birthplace. |
| 20 | **No place death cannot reach**<br>དེ་ནས་ཡིད་རྟེན་མེད་པ་ལ་བསམ་སྟེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | Mountain peak, mid-ocean, mid-air: there is no location, wealth, or arrangement that death cannot enter. Safety planning has a boundary; find it. |
| 21 | **By any circumstance**<br>དེ་ནས་རྐྱེན་སྣང་ངེས་མེད་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | Even without catastrophe, death approaches like the evening shadow — and food, clothing, and pleasure themselves can kill. "When shall I die?" — from the depths of the heart. |
| 22 | **Fierce resolve: death at every act**<br>དེ་ནས་འདུན་པ་དྲག་པོ་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ་མི་རྟག་པ་ནི། | The stage's culmination: at every departure, every meal, every lying down — "this may be the last act of this life." Meditate fervently on death alone. Gate day. |

**Working the stage:** this is the ambush-notification stage — randomized death-reminders at genuinely arbitrary moments enact "the time is unknown" as no scheduled session can. Day 22's practice (death-awareness attached to ordinary acts) is then *sustained by notifications for the rest of the plan*.

## Stage 3 — The Waterwheel · days 23–38
**Chapter 3: The sufferings of saṃsāra · manual topic 3**

**Transformation goal:** honest, felt disillusionment with every samsaric strategy, high or low. **Measure:** "If when it's explained you are not moved to sadness... It's clear you have no mind at all!" **Gate question (day 38):** "Name one strategy you were counting on to finally make you safe or happy. Do you still believe it?"

| Day | Session | The day's work |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | **General weariness**<br>དོན་གསུམ་པ་འཁོར་བ་སྡུག་བསྔལ་དུ་བསམ་པ་ལ་གསུམ་སྟེ། | The panorama: bodies of past births towering over Meru, tears drunk exceeding the ocean; this life's gains vanishing at death like a dream on waking. |
| 24 | **The eight hot hells**<br>དེ་ནས་སྡུག་བསྔལ་བྱེ་བྲག་ཏུ་བསམ་པ་ལ་དྲུག་ལས༑ དམྱལ་བའང་ཚ་དམྱལ་བརྒྱད་ཀྱི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | Contemplate the eight hot hells one by one, each with vastly greater intensity and duration; resolve to do whatever is needed never to be born there. |
| 25 | **The neighboring hells**<br>དེ་ནས་ཉེ་འཁོར་བའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ལས། ཉེ་འཁོར་བ་ནི། | The sixteen surrounding hells — the ember pit, the corpse swamp, the razor road, the river of ash — met and re-met many times before. Resolve definite escape. |
| 26 | **The eight cold hells**<br>དེ་ནས་གྲང་དམྱལ་བརྒྱད་ཀྱི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | The cold hells of ice and blizzard, named for the cries and the splitting of the frozen body. Rejoice at not being there; resolve to strive. |
| 27 | **The ephemeral hells**<br>དེ་ནས་ཉི་ཚེ་བའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | Beings pressed inside rocks, boiled in springs, born as used objects — suffering hidden inside the ordinary world's furniture. |
| 28 | **The pretas**<br>དེ་ནས་ཡི་དགས་ཀྱི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | The three obscurations of craving: oases that vanish on sight, fire in the chest, bodies colonized by their own dependents. Craving's own portrait. |
| 29 | **The animals**<br>དེ་ནས་དུད་འགྲོའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | The dark of the depths, beings packed like dregs devouring one another; and the visible ones — killed, worked, beaten, endlessly afraid. |
| 30 | **Human suffering: the three kinds**<br>དེ་ནས་མིའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི་ | Suffering of suffering (the boil on the sore), of change (the house collapsing on the wedding), of conditioning (the poison already eaten). Modern life mapped precisely. |
| 31 | **Birth**<br>དེ་ནས་སྐྱེ་རྒས་ན་འཆིའི་ཆུ་བོ་བཞི་ལ་བསམ་པ་ནི། | The four rivers begin: the womb's confinement and the trauma of birth — suffering built into arrival itself. Resolve to exhaust the round of rebirth. |
| 32 | **Old age**<br>དེ་ནས་རྒས་པའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | Strength, senses, memory, and dignity failing; wishing to die while dreading death. Resolve to attain what does not age. |
| 33 | **Sickness**<br>དེ་ནས་ན་བའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ལ་བསམས་ཏེ། | The elements in discord, the body unreliable, the mind despairing, the fear of death arriving early. Resolve to obtain the medicine that frees from all of it. |
| 34 | **Death**<br>དེ་ནས་འཆི་བའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | The last bed revisited — now asking: what use, then, were the enemies subdued, the property protected, the position defended? Practice the deathless path. |
| 35 | **Meeting, parting, and the aggregates**<br>དེ་ནས་མི་སྡུག་པ་དང་ཕྲད་པའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | Meeting what you dread, losing what you love — and beneath both, the five aggregates themselves as the standing infrastructure of pain. |
| 36 | **The asuras**<br>དེ་ནས་ལྷ་མ་ཡིན་གྱི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | Beings consumed by comparison: unable to bear others' splendor, permanently at war. Envy's own portrait. Resolve on the peaceful Dharma. |
| 37 | **The gods**<br>དེ་ནས་ལྷའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ནི། | Even the best-case scenario ends: the gods foresee their fall as merit runs out. Comfort is not exit. Resolve to escape the whole wheel. |
| 38 | **Inference from present experience**<br>དེ་ནས་ད་ལྟའི་ཚུལ་ལ་དཔག་པ་ནི། | The stage's empirical close: you cannot bear a spark on the skin or a day's illness now — measure what that implies. Gate day. |

**Working the stage:** the feed's self-referential moment — the moth-and-candle verse displayed inside the mechanism it describes. The "heart of sadness" framing (sadness as noble wealth, not depression) runs through all stage copy, with Khyentse's caution against indulgence kept explicit. Days 30–35 are the stage's modern center of gravity; the realm days (24–29, 36–37) are taught as both cosmology and phenomenology of craving, envy, and comfort, without flattening the tradition's realism about rebirth.

## Stage 4 — The Shadow · days 39–46
**Chapter 4: The karmic law of cause and effect · manual topic 4**

**Transformation goal:** conviction in infallible causality, expressed in conduct. **Measure:** treating nonvirtues "as the enemies they are" — adopting and rejecting without sliding into nihilism. **Gate question (day 46):** "Which one act did you refuse, and which one did you choose, this week *because* of cause and effect?"

| Day | Session | The day's work |
|---|---|---|
| 39 | **The three physical non-virtues**<br>དང་པོ་མི་དགེ་བའི་ལས་བཅུའི་ལུས་ཀྱི་ལས་གསུམ་ནི། | Killing, stealing, sexual misconduct — each traced to its concordant results in this life and beyond. Resolve to abandon them from today. |
| 40 | **The four verbal non-virtues**<br>དེ་ནས་ངག་གི་བཞི་ནི། | Lying, divisive talk, harsh words, idle chatter — and the social world each one builds around the speaker. Resolve to abandon them. |
| 41 | **The three mental non-virtues**<br>དེ་ནས་ཡིད་ཀྱི་གསུམ་ནི་ | Covetousness, malice, wrong views — the failures, fears, and blindness they ripen into. Resolve to abandon them. |
| 42 | **The three physical virtues**<br>དེ་ནས་དགེ་བའི་ལས་ལ། | The mirror image: what protecting life, giving, and fidelity build. Resolve to rely on them. |
| 43 | **The four verbal virtues**<br>དེ་ནས་ངག་གི་བཞི་ནི། རྫུན་སྤངས་པས་ཀུན་གྱིས་བསྟོད་ཅིང་དགའ་བར་བྱེད་པ་དང་། | Truthful, harmonizing, gentle, meaningful speech — a voice that is trusted, welcome, and worth keeping. Resolve to rely on them. |
| 44 | **The three mental virtues**<br>དེ་ནས་ཡིད་ཀྱི་ལས་གསུམ་ནི། | Contentment, goodwill, right view — hopes fulfilled, freedom from fear, the view made excellent. Resolve to take them up. |
| 45 | **Aiming virtue at liberation**<br>དེ་ནས་ཐར་པ་ཆ་མཐུན་གྱི་ལས་ནི། | Redirect all virtue toward liberation, sealed with the three: bodhichitta before, non-reifying focus during, dedication after — the plan's session formula taught as karma. |
| 46 | **Karma as the painter**<br>ལས་འདི་ནི་རི་མོ་མཁན་དང་འདྲ་བ། | The stage's synthesis in similes: karma the painter of all forms, the shadow that follows, the untransferable, the unmixing, the unfailing. Know everything as karma. Gate day. |

**Working the stage:** the counter debuts — counting kept adopt-and-reject commitments (days 39–44 each seed one), checked in the evening review. Day 45 is a hinge: it teaches the three-part session shape *as itself the karma of liberation*, which the timer has been enacting since day 1.

## Stage 5 — The Guide · days 47–55
**Chapter 5: The spiritual master · manual topic 5**

**Transformation goal:** trust in guidance — the shift from consuming spiritual content to following a path. **Measure:** "All existence / Will arise as your own teacher." **Gate question (day 55):** "Do you want a living teacher? What would you look for, and what would make you walk away?"

| Day | Session | The day's work |

Both must be resolved when the manual is promoted to `1-SOURCES/` with block IDs. Minor scribal variants in the ordinals (e.g. ལྔ་པའོ for ང་ལྔ་པའོ at kr 55) do not affect numbering.
2. **Depth-reduction days.** Days 53–55 (guru-yoga cycle) and day 54's activity-mantras are taught at contemplation/aspiration depth per the strategy's no-simulated-guru boundary; the full practices are flagged in-app as belonging with a living teacher. This is a deliberate curriculum decision, recorded here so translators and reviewers do not "correct" it back to full depth.
3. **What each day file needs** (per the Plans convention): the khrid rkang unit (bo, transcluded), its en rendering (from the Translation track), the corresponding root-text verses (from verse rails), the session script for the timer (three-part structure), the day's notification pool, the feed cards, and the journal prompt. Gate days additionally carry the stage self-assessment.
4. **Liturgical dependencies.** Days 58 (refuge formula), 66–73 (seven branches and vow), and 75 (tonglen) require fixed recitation texts in the practitioner's language — these come from the Translation track, not ad-hoc rendering, and are reused daily thereafter (day 58's formula; day 72's dedication).
